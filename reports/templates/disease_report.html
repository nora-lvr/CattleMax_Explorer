A case is a run of therapeutic treatments on one animal for one disease — treatments closer together than the episode gap are the same case.

Trend across cohorts

Each point is a group that entered the phase together, so a Spring and a Fall calving are never averaged into one number that describes neither. One panel per disease, each on its own scale — the patterns differ far more than the levels do. Hollow points are cohorts still in the phase, whose figures will rise.

How much disease, by production phase

The headline number is the attack rate — the share of animals entering a phase that had at least one case during it. This is deliberately not a per-day rate: risk is not spread evenly through a phase, so a per-day figure understates diseases that strike early.

When in the phase disease strikes

Cases by day of phase — days since birth for calves, days since weaning for growers. This is what makes the timing actionable: two diseases with a similar attack rate can need completely different intervention points.

These timing charts use only animals that were here for the event the clock starts on. An animal bought at eighteen months has no weaning we witnessed, so her day 40 is forty days after she arrived, not forty days after weaning — mixing the two would smear the curve. Those animals are dropped from both the cases and the animals-at-risk, never from one side only, and are counted below. They remain in every attack rate on this page.

What this report does not know

Nothing has been discarded to produce these figures. Treatments with no diagnosis recorded are reported as Unknown disease rather than dropped, so they still count against every rate. Every record excluded anywhere in the pipeline is listed in the exclusions ledger that ships beside the data.
