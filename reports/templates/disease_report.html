Disease incidence, timing and outcome

Every therapeutic treatment on the operation, grouped into disease cases rather than counted as treatments, and placed against the number of animals actually at risk in each production phase.

A case is a run of therapeutic treatments on one animal for one disease. Treatments closer together than the episode gap are the same case; a longer break starts a new one. Counting treatments instead would inflate every figure by the re-treatment rate.

The groups on the ground now

The most recent cohort to enter each phase — the calves, growers and cows currently moving through. These groups are still in their phase, so their rates are a floor and will rise as they age. They are the ones you can still act on.

Trend across cohorts

Each point is a group that entered the phase together. Comparing groups rather than calendar years keeps a Spring and a Fall calving from being averaged into one meaningless number. Hollow points are cohorts still in the phase.

How much disease, by production phase

The headline number is the attack rate — the share of animals entering a phase that had at least one case during it. This is deliberately not a per-day rate: risk is not spread evenly through a phase, so a per-day figure understates diseases that strike early.

When in the phase disease strikes

Cases by day of phase — days since birth for calves, days since weaning for growers. This is what makes the timing actionable: two diseases with a similar attack rate can need completely different intervention points.

What this report does not know

Nothing has been discarded to produce these figures. Treatments with no diagnosis recorded are reported as Unknown disease rather than dropped, so they still count against every rate. Every record excluded anywhere in the pipeline is listed in the exclusions ledger that ships beside the data.
